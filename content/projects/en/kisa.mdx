## The problem

Most budgeting apps want a bank login and a cloud account before they show you a single number. Their dashboards throw everything at the wall. And almost none of them speak Kurdish, or handle a currency like the Iraqi dinar without rounding it into nonsense.

I wanted an expense tracker that stays out of the way: one glance, one number, and no server that ever sees a transaction.

## What I built

Kisa is an expense tracker for iPhone that keeps the whole picture on one screen — balance, cash flow, budgets, upcoming bills, and recent activity — and hides everything else until you ask for it.

You add money by hand, by voice, or by scanning a receipt; the OCR runs on the device. Budgets, recurring plans, accounts, categories, insights, and home-screen widgets are all built in, and CSV or Excel export is always free so your data is never locked in.

Every record lives in a local SQLite database. If you turn on sync it goes to your own private iCloud through CloudKit, never to a server I run. Couples can share individual accounts — "yours, mine, ours" — and settle up, while everything else stays private to each person.

The app ships in English, Arabic, Spanish, and both Kurdish dialects: Sorani in right-to-left and Kurmanji in left-to-right, with amounts, calendars, and layouts that follow each locale.

Kisa Pro is a StoreKit subscription that unlocks unlimited scans, sync, sharing, and PDF reports.
